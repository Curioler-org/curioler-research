    <h1 class="about-title">About Curioler Research</h1>

    <section class="about-section">
      <h2>What this is</h2>
      <p>Curioler Research is a growing library of plain-language research summaries and evidence checks on autism — written for caregivers, not clinicians. Every summary is grounded in peer-reviewed evidence, rated by evidence tier, and structured so you can find what you need quickly.</p>
      <p>This site is part of <strong>Curioler</strong> — a broader platform being built at <a href="https://curioler.com">curioler.com</a> to help families of autistic children navigate research, interventions, and everyday decisions with more confidence. The research published here feeds directly into that platform and serves as its knowledge repository.</p>
    </section>

    <section class="about-section">
      <h2>Who built it</h2>
      <p>Curioler is built by <strong>Bhavin Solanki</strong>, a data scientist and AI practitioner working at the intersection of data science, clinical research, and product thinking. Bhavin holds an MSc in Biostatistics and has spent his career applying data and AI to real-world problems in health and research.</p>
      <p>Curioler grew out of a personal need — the experience of navigating autism research as a caregiver and finding that most of it was either inaccessible, buried behind paywalls, or written in language that assumed a clinical background. This site is an attempt to change that.</p>
      <p>You can reach Bhavin at <a href="mailto:strikeagle.lx@gmail.com">strikeagle.lx@gmail.com</a>.</p>
    </section>

    <section class="about-section">
      <h2>How AI is used</h2>
      <p>Every summary and myth check on this site is researched and structured with the assistance of AI — specifically Claude (by Anthropic) running within Claude Code. The AI searches the web for peer-reviewed sources, extracts structured information, detects content type, and drafts the plain-language write-up.</p>
      <p>All published content is reviewed before going live. The AI does not fabricate sources — every link points to a real paper or resource. However, AI can make mistakes: it may misinterpret a study's findings, overstate certainty, or miss important context. If you spot an error, please get in touch.</p>
      <p><strong>This site is not a substitute for professional advice.</strong> The summaries here are designed to help you understand research — not to replace the guidance of a qualified clinician, therapist, or specialist who knows your child.</p>
    </section>

    <section class="about-section">
      <h2>Evidence tiers</h2>
      <p>Every summary is rated by the strength of its evidence:</p>
      <ul class="tier-list">
        <li><span class="tier-badge tier-1">Tier 1</span> Systematic review or meta-analysis — the strongest form of evidence</li>
        <li><span class="tier-badge tier-2">Tier 2</span> Peer-reviewed study</li>
        <li><span class="tier-badge tier-3">Tier 3</span> Clinical consensus or guideline</li>
        <li><span class="tier-badge tier-4">Tier 4</span> Expert commentary or opinion</li>
      </ul>
    </section>

    <section class="about-section">
      <h2>Myth check verdicts</h2>
      <p>Each myth check assigns one of five verdicts:</p>
      <ul class="tier-list">
        <li><span class="verdict-badge verdict-myth">Myth</span> Clearly contradicted by strong evidence</li>
        <li><span class="verdict-badge verdict-supported">Supported</span> Well-supported by strong evidence</li>
        <li><span class="verdict-badge verdict-nuanced">Nuanced</span> Partially true or highly context-dependent</li>
        <li><span class="verdict-badge verdict-misleading">Misleading</span> Contains truth but leads to wrong conclusions</li>
        <li><span class="verdict-badge verdict-insufficient-evidence">Insufficient evidence</span> Not enough quality research to say either way</li>
      </ul>
    </section>
