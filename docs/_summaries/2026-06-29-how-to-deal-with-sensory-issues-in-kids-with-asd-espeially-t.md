# Understanding Sensory Issues and Humming in Autistic Children: What Caregivers Need to Know

**Source:** Patil O, Kaple M. Sensory Processing Differences in Individuals With Autism Spectrum Disorder: A Narrative Review of Underlying Mechanisms and Sensory-Based Interventions. *Cureus.* 2023; PMC10687592. (Supplemented by clinical guidance from Hopebridge Autism Therapy Centers, Autism Parenting Magazine, Rise Up For Autism, and New Patterns ABA.)
**Evidence level:** Tier 2 — Peer-Reviewed Study
**Domain:** Sensory

## What this research found

- **Sensory processing differences are very common in autism.** Between 53% and 95% of autistic children show some degree of difference in how they process sensory information — meaning their brains respond more strongly (or less strongly) to everyday sights, sounds, textures, smells, and more.

- **Humming is a form of "vocal stimming" — and it serves a real purpose.** Children who hum repeatedly are often using the sound to self-regulate: to calm down when overwhelmed, block out uncomfortable sensory input, maintain focus, or express excitement or emotion. It is not misbehaviour — it is a coping tool.

- **The brain science behind it is real.** Research shows that autistic individuals have differences in how their brains filter and modulate incoming sensory information (known as sensory gating). This means sounds, textures, and other stimuli can feel far more intense than they would for a neurotypical person, making self-soothing behaviours like humming genuinely helpful.

- **Trying to stop or suppress stimming can cause harm.** Multiple sources agree that abruptly suppressing vocal stimming can increase distress, damage self-esteem, and reduce a child's sense of control. The goal should be understanding and gentle shaping — not elimination.

- **Sensory-based therapies show promise.** Sensory Integration Therapy (SIT) delivered by occupational therapists, along with ABA therapy, environmental modifications (like quieter spaces and noise-reducing headphones), and sensory tools (like fidget toys or weighted items) have all shown benefit in helping children manage sensory challenges more comfortably.

## What this means for caregivers of autistic children

- **Try to see the humming as communication.** When your child hums — especially during meals, transitions, or busy environments — it is likely their nervous system's way of coping. Noticing *when* it increases can help you identify what might be overwhelming them and make adjustments before things escalate.

- **Create space for it, and plan around it.** Rather than trying to eliminate humming entirely, consider when and where it is okay to let it happen freely. A quiet corner at home, noise-reducing headphones in busy places, or a gentle heads-up before a noisy situation can reduce the need for it in the first place.

- **Work *with* therapists, not around them.** Occupational therapists and speech-language pathologists can carry out a sensory profile assessment to understand exactly what your child's triggers are. They can build a personalised plan — which might include sensory-rich activities, alternative outlets, and communication strategies — to help your child manage their sensory world more comfortably.

- **Be patient with yourself too.** If your child's humming is constant and you find it hard to tune out, that is completely understandable. Recognising your own sensory limits and finding moments to recharge is part of being a sustainable caregiver.

## Important caveats

- **This review is a narrative review, not a systematic one.** That means the authors selected studies to discuss rather than conducting a comprehensive, bias-controlled analysis of all available evidence. The findings are informative but should be interpreted with some caution — this is Tier 2 evidence, not the highest level available.

- **Every child is different.** Sensory profiles vary enormously between autistic children. What works brilliantly for one child (weighted blankets, background music, sensory bins) may not work for another. There is no one-size-fits-all approach.

- **If humming or other sensory behaviours are significantly disrupting your child's daily life, learning, or safety, please seek professional assessment.** An occupational therapist, paediatrician, or ABA specialist can help identify what is driving the behaviour and tailor support accordingly. This summary is not a substitute for that personalised guidance.

## About this research

The primary source is a narrative review published in the peer-reviewed journal *Cureus* (2023), in which researchers searched PubMed and the Cochrane Library for studies on sensory processing differences in autism, covering literature from 1999 to 2023. The review examined the neurological mechanisms behind sensory processing differences — including altered brain connectivity and sensory gating dysfunction — as well as the effectiveness of sensory-based therapies. It was supplemented here by clinical practitioner guidance and expert commentary from Hopebridge Autism Therapy Centers, Autism Parenting Magazine, Rise Up For Autism, and New Patterns ABA, which provided applied context on vocal stimming and caregiver strategies.
