BACKGROUND: Executive function (EF) refers to the cognitive processes that enable goal-directed regulation of thoughts, emotions, and behaviors. Two interrelated domains are commonly distinguished: "cool" EF, which involves abstract cognitive processes, and "hot" EF, which involves regulation in emotionally or motivationally charged contexts. Children with special educational needs (SEN) frequently show difficulties across both domains, yet most interventions target one in isolation.
OBJECTIVE: The present study aims to evaluate the effectiveness of a dual EF training package for 3 of the most prevalent SEN groups: attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD), specific learning difficulties (SpLD), and autism spectrum disorder (ASD).
METHODS: A total of 288 primary school students aged 6 to 12 years with a diagnosis of ADHD, ASD, or SpLD will be recruited. Participants will be randomly assigned to either an intervention or a waitlist control group, stratified by diagnosis. Each intervention group (n=48) will receive the dual EF training program. Participants in the waitlist control group will continue to receive usual educational and clinical support (standard care) during the study period and will be offered the dual EF program upon completion of the study. Preintervention and postintervention assessments will include neuropsychological tests of EF, teacher- and parent-reported behavioral ratings, and measures of disability impact, school satisfaction, and social participation. Changes in outcome measures will be examined using a mixed-design analysis, with diagnosis (ADHD, ASD, and SpLD) and group (experimental vs waitlist control) as between-participant factors and time (pretest vs posttest) as the within-participant factor. Baseline scores will be included as covariates where appropriate. The analysis will assess overall intervention effects and whether changes over time differ by intervention condition and diagnosis, with significant interactions followed by appropriate post hoc comparisons.
RESULTS: The study was funded by Advance Children Medical and Education Foundation Limited from September 2025 to December 2026 and received ethical approval. Recruitment commenced in early 2026, and 30 participants with ADHD, 30 with ASD, and 45 with SpLD had been enrolled by June 2026. Baseline assessments were completed for all participants. Further data collection and data analysis are scheduled for completion by early 2027, with the results to be submitted for publication thereafter.
CONCLUSIONS: This study will evaluate the effectiveness of a theme-based dual EF program that integrates hot and cool EF within a structured curriculum. Findings will provide evidence of the potential of this approach to support EF and related outcomes in children with SEN and may inform future research and inclusive educational practices.
