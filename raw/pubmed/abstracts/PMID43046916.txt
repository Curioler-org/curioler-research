Autism spectrum disorders (ASD) are a heterogeneous group of conditions in which neuroinflammation, systemic inflammation, and impaired mitochondrial energy function (mitochondrial dysfunction) serve as interconnected pathogenetic factors. Using organic acids profiling in urine it is possible to assess metabolic alterations related to glycolysis, the tricarboxylic acid (TCA) cycle, fatty acid β-oxidation, ketogenesis, branched-chain amino acid (BCAA) catabolism, and coenzyme Q10 metabolism. In this study we have analyzed 26 such metabolites in children with ASD and a control group of neurotypical children. The objective was to identify and characterize metabolic variants of mitochondrial dysfunction in ASD children and to evaluate the impact of the microbiota to the urinary organic acid profile. This single-center, observational, cross-sectional study included 105 ASD children (aged 3-9 years) and 95 conditionally healthy, neurotypical children, matched for age and sex. Urinary organic acids were measured using gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). Children with ASD exhibited diverse, bidirectional alterations in metabolites associated with energy metabolism. In the overall clinical group, there were elevated levels of pyruvic, cis-aconitic, isocitric, fumaric, and sebacic acids, as well as several intermediates of BCAA catabolism, and decreased levels of 2-ketoglutaric and succinic acids. Cluster analysis resulted in the identification of two distinct metabolic variants. The first cluster included 58 children and was characterized by the hyperexcretion of most organic acids, a pronounced alteration in TCA cycle markers, elevated meglutol levels, and a higher density within the metabolic correlation network. The second cluster, comprising 47 children, showed a metabolic profile similar to that of the control group, with reduced excretion of several key metabolites - primarily those associated with the TCA cycle and coenzyme Q10 metabolism. The primary contributors to the separation between these clusters were cis-aconitic, isocitric, citric, 2-ketoglutaric, malic, and fumaric acids, as well as meglutol. Urinary organic acids profiling enabled the identification of two patterns of metabolic dysregulation: hyperexcretory and hypoexcretory. These patterns reflect varying states of mitochondrial dysfunction, ranging from compensatory to subcompensated or decompensated. Subsequent comparison of the identified patterns of metabolic dysregulation with the functional status of mitochondria requires longitudinal monitoring of clinical condition of patients and also the assessment of inflammatory markers, such as pro-inflammatory cytokines, quinolinic acid, and kynurenic acid.
